# Introduction And Related Work

Use this reference for Introduction, Background, Related Work, literature review, and any literature-heavy passage.

## Evidence Channel

Apply `literature-survey` for all published-paper evidence.

- Source-specific factual sentences cite `source="literature"` Grounds with `\cite{statement_N}`.
- Paragraph theses are Claims, not citations.
- Synthesis across papers requires a Warrant.
- Conflicting or boundary-setting literature becomes Rebuttal, not vague hedging.

Do not enumerate papers unless the enumeration serves a Claim.

## Introduction Shape

An Introduction usually advances a chain of Claims:

1. field-level problem or phenomenon
2. known evidence and limitations
3. unresolved gap
4. this manuscript's motivating Claim or question

Each step must be grounded. The gap Claim must not be broader than the cited literature supports.

## Related Work Shape

Related Work should organize evidence by argument function, not by paper order.

Use separate Claims for:

- consensus findings
- method families or conceptual categories
- known limitations
- unresolved disagreement
- the niche this manuscript occupies

A taxonomy or grouping is a Claim unless directly quoted from a source. Support it with literature Grounds and a Warrant explaining why the grouping is valid.

## Gap Discipline

Do not write "little is known" or "no studies have..." unless search evidence supports that absence. If the gap is based on literature search, record the search result as evidence or narrow the gap Claim.

If there is contradictory literature, the gap should become scoped rather than absolute.

## Completion

The section is complete when:

- every factual paper statement cites a verified literature Ground
- every synthesis paragraph has a Claim and Warrant
- conflicts and limitations are Rebuttals
- the stated gap is supported by the cited evidence and search scope
