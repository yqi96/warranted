# Results

Use this reference for Results prose, figure/table-linked paragraphs, result-bearing captions, and source-data references.

## Result Paragraph Contract

Each result paragraph must be anchored to observed Grounds.

Before writing the paragraph:

- identify the paragraph Claim
- identify the observed Ground(s) that support it
- identify the figure, table, source data, script, or output artifact attached to each Ground
- identify the Warrant that licenses the inference from those results to the Claim

Do not use literature Grounds alone to support a Results paragraph Claim.

## Figure And Table Discipline

Figures and tables are evidence interfaces, not arguments by themselves.

- Each result-bearing panel, row, or statistic should map to one or more observed Grounds.
- Captions must not claim more than the linked Grounds establish.
- A visual trend is not enough if the Claim depends on a quantified effect; create or verify the quantitative Ground.
- Source data must be attachable when a figure/table is used as evidence.

## Scope And Strength

If a result comes from a subset, pilot, approximation, exploratory endpoint, failed assumption, or underpowered comparison:

- record the scope in the observed Ground, Backing limitation, Rebuttal, or unresolved obligation
- do not write an unscoped Claim
- do not let a narrow figure panel support a broad section thesis without a Warrant

## Negative And Null Results

Negative, null, or failed results are still evidence.

- If they contradict an expected Ground or Claim implication, audit the discrepancy before Rebuttal.
- If they define a boundary condition, create a Rebuttal.
- If they are inconclusive, keep the Ground pending and name the next obligation.

## Completion

The Results section is complete when:

- every result paragraph has a Claim
- every result Claim is supported by observed Grounds and artifacts
- figure/table captions match the linked Grounds
- scoped results remain scoped in prose
- unexpected mismatches are audited before verdict language
